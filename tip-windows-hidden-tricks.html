5 Hidden Windows 11 Tricks You Need to Know

Windows 11 is packed with features, but Microsoft hides some of the best productivity hacks deep inside the OS. Here are 5 hidden tricks that will completely change how you use your PC.

1. The Secret Clipboard History (Win + V)

Stop going back and forth to copy and paste multiple items. Windows has a built-in clipboard manager that remembers your last 25 copied items (including images!).

Just press Windows Key + V. The first time you do it, it will ask you to "Turn on" Clipboard History. Once enabled, whenever you press Win+V, a menu pops up allowing you to paste anything you copied earlier that day.

2. Shake to Minimize (Aero Shake)

Have 15 windows open and just want to focus on one? You can literally grab the window you want to keep and "shake" it to minimize everything else.

This is disabled by default in Windows 11. To turn it on: Go to Settings > System > Multitasking and toggle on Title bar window shake. Now, click and hold the title bar of a window and jiggle your mouse quickly back and forth.

3. The Emoji & Symbol Picker (Win + .)

Need to type an emoji on your laptop, or insert a special symbol like a degree sign (°), em-dash (—), or currency symbol (€)? You don't need to Google them and copy-paste.

Press Windows Key + Period (.). A panel will pop up containing emojis, GIFs, kaomoji ( ¯\_(ツ)_/¯ ), and all special mathematical and punctuation symbols.

4. Secret Start Menu (Win + X)

Power users hate digging through the modern Settings app to find traditional tools like Device Manager, Disk Management, or the Terminal.

Right-click the Start Button, or press Windows Key + X. You get an instant, no-nonsense menu to access every important administrative tool in Windows.

5. Snap Layouts (Hover over Maximize)

Windows 11 has the best window management of any OS, but people drag windows to the edge of the screen like it's 2015.

Simply hover your mouse over the Maximize button (the square next to the X) on any window. A grid will appear. Click which zone you want that window to snap into, and Windows will automatically arrange the rest of your open apps into the remaining slots.
