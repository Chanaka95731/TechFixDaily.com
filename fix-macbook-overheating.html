MacBook Overheating Fix: Stay Cool While Working

Is your MacBook sounding like a jet engine ready for takeoff? Overheating is a massive issue for older Intel Macs, and even occasionally affects the newer M-series chips if a background process goes rogue. Excessive heat degrades your battery health and slows down performance (thermal throttling).

Fix 1: Check Activity Monitor for Runaway Apps

Often, a single app getting stuck in a loop causes 100% CPU usage, generating massive heat.

  1. Press Cmd + Space to open Spotlight, type Activity Monitor, and hit Enter.
  2. Click on the CPU tab at the top.
  3. Click the % CPU column header to sort by highest usage.
  4. If you see an app consistently taking over 70-80% CPU and you aren't actively using it, select it and click the X icon at the top to force quit.

Fix 2: Reset the SMC (Intel Macs Only)

The System Management Controller (SMC) manages battery and thermal management (fans). Resetting it often fixes fan issues. Note: M1/M2/M3 Macs do not have an SMC to reset.

For MacBooks with T2 Security Chip (2018-2020):

  1. Shut down your Mac.
  2. Press and hold Control (left) + Option (left) + Shift (right) for 7 seconds.
  3. Keep holding those keys, and also press and hold the Power button for 7 more seconds.
  4. Release all keys, wait a few seconds, and turn the Mac back on.

Fix 3: Elevate Your MacBook

MacBooks (especially the Air) use the aluminum chassis to dissipate heat. If you use it on a bed, pillow, or your lap, it traps the heat entirely.

Always use your Mac on a hard, flat surface (like a desk). If you push it hard with video editing, invest in a cheap laptop stand to improve airflow underneath the chassis.

Fix 4: Control Your Fans Manually

macOS prefers a silent laptop over a cool laptop, meaning it waits until the Mac is very hot before spinning up the fans.

  1. Download a free app called Macs Fan Control.
  2. Install and open the app.
  3. You can set a "Sensor-based" rule to make the fans spin up earlier, keeping the chassis much cooler at the expense of a little bit of fan noise.

Fix 5: Close Endless Browser Tabs

Google Chrome is notorious for eating RAM and CPU on macOS. If you have 50 tabs open, your Mac is working incredibly hard to keep them all active.

Use Safari (which is far more optimized for macOS batteries and thermals) or use extensions like "The Great Suspender" in Chrome to put unused tabs to sleep.
