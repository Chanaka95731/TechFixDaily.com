WiFi Keeps Dropping? Complete Fix Guide 2025

There is nothing more annoying than a WiFi connection that randomly drops while you're in a Zoom meeting or gaming. If your WiFi keeps disconnecting, the issue usually boils down to router placement, channel interference, or an outdated network driver. Let's fix it.

Fix 1: The Golden Router Restart

It sounds like a cliché, but restarting the router fixes IP conflict issues 80% of the time.

  1. Unplug the power cable from the back of your router (don't just press the power button).
  2. Wait for exactly 30 seconds. This allows the capacitors to drain completely.
  3. Plug it back in and wait 2-3 minutes for the lights to stabilize.

Fix 2: Forget and Reconnect (Windows & Mac)

Sometimes the saved network profile gets corrupted on your device.

For Windows:

  1. Settings > Network & internet > Wi-Fi > Manage known networks.
  2. Click Forget next to your network name.
  3. Click the WiFi icon in the taskbar and reconnect (you will need to enter the password again).

For Mac:

  1. System Settings > Wi-Fi > Advanced (or Details).
  2. Click the three dots next to the network > Remove From List.
  3. Reconnect.

Fix 3: Change DNS Servers

Your ISP's default DNS server might be failing, causing the internet to drop even if the WiFi stays connected.

  1. On Windows: Win + R > type ncpa.cpl > Enter.
  2. Right-click your Wi-Fi adapter > Properties.
  3. Double-click Internet Protocol Version 4 (TCP/IPv4).
  4. Select "Use the following DNS server addresses".
  5. Set Preferred to 8.8.8.8 and Alternate to 8.8.4.4 (Google DNS) or 1.1.1.1 (Cloudflare).
  6. Click OK.

Fix 4: Update Network Adapter Drivers (Windows)

Outdated drivers are a primary cause of random disconnections on Windows laptops.

  1. Right-click the Start button > Device Manager.
  2. Expand Network adapters.
  3. Right-click your Wi-Fi adapter (usually says Intel, Realtek, or Broadcom) > Update driver.
  4. Choose "Search automatically for drivers".
  5. Pro tip: If updating doesn't work, try selecting "Roll back driver" via the Properties tab.

Fix 5: Stop Windows from Turning Off Your WiFi

Windows has a power-saving feature that intentionally turns off your WiFi adapter to save battery.

  1. Open Device Manager > Network adapters > Right-click your Wi-Fi adapter > Properties.
  2. Go to the Power Management tab.
  3. Uncheck the box that says "Allow the computer to turn off this device to save power".
  4. Click OK.

Fix 6: Split the 2.4GHz and 5GHz Bands

Modern routers combine the 2.4GHz and 5GHz bands into a single network name (Smart Connect). Sometimes, your device gets confused switching between them, causing drops.

You need to log into your router's admin panel (usually 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1), go to Wireless Settings, disable "Smart Connect", and rename your networks to MyWiFi_2G and MyWiFi_5G. Connect to the 5G band if you are close to the router, and 2G if you are far away.
