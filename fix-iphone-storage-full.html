iPhone Storage Full? Free Up Space Without Deleting Photos

There's nothing worse than trying to take a photo of a special moment, only to get the dreaded "Storage Full" popup. If you have a 64GB or 128GB iPhone, managing storage is a constant battle. Here is how to quickly reclaim gigabytes of space without deleting your precious memories.

Fix 1: Offload Unused Apps

This is Apple's best storage feature. It deletes the app itself (freeing up massive amounts of space) but keeps your personal data and documents. If you ever want the app back, you just redownload it and it's exactly how you left it.

  1. Go to Settings > General > iPhone Storage.
  2. Tap on Offload Unused Apps to enable it automatically, OR
  3. Scroll down, tap on a specific heavy app you rarely use (like a 2GB game), and tap Offload App.

Fix 2: Optimize iPhone Photos

If your photo library is taking up 40GB+, you need to use iCloud Optimization. It keeps full-resolution photos in iCloud, and smaller, low-res thumbnails on your phone until you tap on them.

  1. Go to Settings > [Your Name] > iCloud > Photos.
  2. Ensure "Sync this iPhone" is turned on.
  3. Check the box for Optimize iPhone Storage.
  4. Note: You may need to upgrade your iCloud storage to 50GB ($0.99/mo) for this to work effectively.

Fix 3: Clear iMessage Attachments

Do you text videos, photos, and GIFs to your friends? iMessage saves every single one of them forever by default, taking up massive amounts of space.

  1. Go to Settings > General > iPhone Storage.
  2. Scroll down and tap on Messages.
  3. Tap on Review Large Attachments.
  4. Tap Edit in the top right, select the massive videos you no longer need, and hit the trash can.

Fix 4: Clear Safari Cache and Reading List

Over time, Safari stores hundreds of megabytes of website data and offline reading lists.

  1. Go to Settings > Safari.
  2. Scroll down and tap Clear History and Website Data.
  3. To clear the offline reading list: Go to Settings > General > iPhone Storage > Safari and swipe left on "Offline Reading List" to delete it.

Fix 5: Delete Recently Deleted Items

When you delete a photo, it doesn't actually leave your phone. It sits in a "Recently Deleted" folder for 30 days, just in case you change your mind.

To permanently free up the space immediately, go to Photos > Albums > Recently Deleted (at the very bottom), tap Select, and tap Delete All.
